StrokeSmriti privacy policy

Last updated 19 November 2026.

StrokeSmriti is a free tool that clinicians use to record a patient's cognitive assessment after a stroke or a stroke procedure. It is a research tool. It is not validated and does not give a diagnosis.

What the app collects

Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else. The app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no server. It works without an internet connection.

What stays on the device

While an assessment is in progress, the answers are kept on the device so that the session survives an accidental close. The app asks for a study ID and does not ask for the patient's name. The in-progress session is deleted once the examiner has copied both the visit code and the results.

Each completed assessment is also saved in a log on the same device, one row per assessment, so that results are not lost if the examiner forgets to copy them. The log holds the study ID, age, sex, education, dates of the stroke and procedure, NIHSS, procedure and imaging details entered by the examiner, and all scores, the examiner's role, pseudonymous examiner code and certification status, any free-text examiner notes, the arousal rating and 4AT score, fatigue and arm-strength ratings, observer-rated mood, apathy and emotionalism ratings, the answer to a safety question on hopelessness or self-harm thoughts, GAD-2, and the reason recorded for each task without a score. Examiners are told not to write names or other identifiers in the notes; sites should check the notes before sharing an export. The log never leaves the device unless the examiner downloads, shares or copies it. The examiner can clear it at any time from the summary screen. Sites should export and clear it regularly under their own data procedures. In the Android app, this data is excluded from device backups.

The visit code and QR

The visit code carries results to the next visit without a server. It is compressed and encoded, not encrypted: anyone with the code and the right software can read it. It contains the study ID, whether the person is a patient or a healthy control, age, sex, years of education, literacy, handedness, lesion side, the index event date (for a healthy control, the date of session 1, with the study purpose, form order and how the person was recruited), the procedure type and date, the patient's language, and the scores of up to four visits recorded as days after the index event. It does not contain the patient's name, visit calendar dates, NIHSS, imaging or procedural details, or the PHQ-9 self-harm item. Treat a printed slip like any clinical record.

What the examiner can copy

The examiner can copy the results as text, download or share the device log as a CSV file, or show the visit code as a QR image. Practice sessions produce no visit code and are not saved to the log. These leave the device only if the examiner pastes, sends or prints them. Handling that copy is the responsibility of the examiner and their institution, under their own consent and ethics procedures.

The read-aloud button uses the device's own text-to-speech voice; the text is processed on the device or by the device's speech service, not by StrokeSmriti.

Camera

The camera is used for two things: reading a visit-code QR when the examiner taps Scan QR, and photographing the paper clock and figure drawings when the examiner taps Photograph. Drawing photos are reduced in size and kept on the device with the assessment in progress, so it can be resumed if interrupted. They leave the device only if the examiner saves or shares them from the summary, and they are deleted from the app when a new assessment starts. The app never sends them anywhere. Examiners must make sure the patient's name or any other identifier is not on the paper before photographing it.

Permissions

The Android app requests internet access only because the app framework requires it, and camera access for scanning visit codes and photographing drawings. The app loads no online content.

Children

The app is meant for use by clinicians with adult patients.

Changes

If a future version offers to upload data, this policy will change before that version is released, and uploading will need ethics approval and patient consent at each site.
